# 6A: making games past AAA with AI agents

A field guide distilled from two months of building five Roblox games, and one web game, with AI coding agents
under one owner who playtests everything. The games: a one-rifle arena shooter, a kids' cooking-and-muscles game
(live, with real player data), a co-op robots-vs-dinos wave shooter, an arm-smashing brawler (live), and a motorbike
sky-road racer. Every rule below came from a failure that was found, measured and fixed. **[M]** = measured
(engine, lab or live players); unmarked numbers were tuned by feel and approved in playtests. A stud is about 0.28 m.

---

## 1. The bar

"6A" means two notches past AAA: **exceptional clarity, responsiveness, tactile animation, cohesive art and reliable
performance, within a small scope.** It is judged against the best games of the current year in the same genre, on the
player's real device (for Roblox: a phone, a 9-to-13-year-old), never graded on effort or on "for an indie / for
Roblox". It passes only at **9/10 or more on every axis**:

| Axis | What a 9 looks like |
|---|---|
| Clarity | A kid knows what to do and what just happened, at phone size, without reading |
| Feel | Input answers in the same frame; motion has weight, anticipation and settle; nothing snaps or floats |
| Tactile animation | Bodies push, lean, grip, squash; no character standing still while things move for it |
| Art | One art direction, a landmark, every object placed for a reason |
| VFX / juice | Rewards travel to where they are counted; effects are rare, short and readable |
| UI / UX | Face up, no modals, one banner at a time, nothing over the character |
| Audio | Every action heard; one voice, one growing piece of music; nothing ringing after the core sound |
| Polish / perf | No flicker, hitch, clip or z-fight; frame time never worse than before; the cheapest phone holds |

**Small and perfect beats big and good.** A game idea must be one sentence a 10-year-old can repeat, with physical,
visible verbs ("eat steaks, get huge, shove everyone off the grill"). Take proven mechanics and change about 20%.

## 2. The ten laws

1. **The whole loop is judged, not your best system.** A technically perfect gun in a grey box was rated "0.1/10".
   One night of polish across everything (a simple real arena, weak bots, hit feedback, sights from a reference,
   a fitted shot sound) turned it into "direction is very right and im already kinda impressed".
2. **Retention is decided in the first 10 minutes.** Live data [M]: next-day return by first-day minutes played:
   under 3 min 4-5%, 3-10 min 8%, 10-20 min 16%, 20-40 min 26-31%, 40+ min 82%. 71% left before minute 10.
   With equal playtime, only the amount of the **core action** done separated returners (15+ cooks 23% vs 11%);
   side modes, daily rewards and come-back items made no difference.
3. **Deliver the thumbnail's promise in minute one.** The store sold "skinny to gigachad", but the muscle growth
   happened only in a side mode most never saw; likes sat at the 17th percentile. Growth on the first steak eaten.
4. **The core skill is the only door to every currency.** When cows out-earned the grill 4:1 and cooking was
   optional, the owner: "why should I cook steaks now". Progression raises what one skill action is worth, not how
   many actions it takes. Stopwatch metrics passing while the game got worse: "stopwatch metrics are not proof of fun".
5. **Restraint.** New effects replace old ones instead of stacking. Protect the core sensation: a "realistic" audio
   pass added shell casings, reverb and tails, and the owner said "weird ringing echo ... very annoying". Nothing
   plays with or right after the core sound until the owner has heard it.
6. **The agent is blind and deaf. Give it instruments.** Labs that run the real code headless, per-frame telemetry,
   filmed takes, measured audio, bots. Never tune by imagining what `stiffness = 420` looks like.
7. **Every critic claim is checked against the frames.** AI video critics are noisy (6/10 and 4/10 on near-identical
   takes) and invent things (3 of 8 claims false in one round). Read claims, not scores.
8. **Match a reference in layout, scale and camera first, then the look.** Three polish rounds on an invented road
   were "nowhere near" the reference; read frame by frame, the real lane was a third as wide, 40 m up, between trees
   10x bigger. Polish never fixes a wrong layout.
9. **Turn every complaint into a machine-checked rule with a number**, run on every build. The owner's map
   complaints became a checker: fake doors and windows 44 → 0, and blind reviewers stopped naming them.
10. **Question the foundation before polishing.** Measure who uses a system before tuning it [M]: card fusing 0%,
    3+ sumo rounds 10%, 0 of 57 sumo-first players returned, a late economy tuned for climbs 10 of 5,946 players
    reached. Cutting beats polishing. (But never lock away the one social thing two early players can do.)

## 3. AI slop: what gives it away

| Signature | Rule |
|---|---|
| Designs written for a reader: classes, currencies, tables ("sounds cool to an AI but not to a human"; "reading it its boring") | Pitch 2-3 hooks: the clip scene first, then the loop, then the catch, with a clear pick |
| Mirrored halves dressed alike, grids of identical boxes, cover dropped to break sightlines ("absolute ai slop", "cluttered for no reason") | Each half its own colours and contents; a landmark per lane; few big pieces, not many small ones |
| Painted doors and windows on solid walls | An opening is real or it is not drawn (a dark pane also reads as an enemy) |
| Every building two storeys with a sniper window | High places are a signature, one per half |
| Rooms dressed by copy-paste (same rug, same lamp) | Every room has a use; contents hug the walls |
| Emoji and symbol glyphs as icons ("look so AI and not 6A") | Painted icons in one house style |
| Flat 2D UI arrows "with lines in them", placeholder podiums | Every visible asset is crafted or it is cut |
| Juice everywhere, flashes by the crosshair, a skin glowing with its own light | Rare (every 1.8-3.4 s), half the first instinct's size, off the aim line and hands |
| Reveals, blinds, countdowns before a decision ("i dislike this") | Deal face up; juice on arrival; never a timer on a purchase |
| Tutorials with fake buttons, a pointing finger over text, narration slower than the tasks ("i hate this tutorial") | A short skippable intro, then words-only hints on the real buttons |
| Reference copied literally (trees, characters) instead of fitted to the game | Fit to the game's palette and play first ("they need to play right as well") |
| Scaling or reshaping a visible weapon part while aiming | Overlays are separate pieces; real mechanical parts stay intact |
| "Premium" pass darkening a map that read well | The bar is bright, readable and saturated with information |

## 4. The loop

Scale effort by what a mistake costs. Most small changes are right first time: quick look, ship to the owner.

| Stakes | Steps |
|---|---|
| Tweak, fix, number | 3, 7 |
| Feature, screen, effect | 0-4, 7 |
| Flagship moment, map, new game, store media | all, and 5 until it holds |

0. **Gate.** Should this exist? Who uses it now? One-sentence kid test? If the request names an existing control
   ("run while holding Q"), it is about that control: look it up and state your reading first.
1. **Spec card**, five lines, vetoable in 30 seconds: the reference (a real clip or picture), the numbers (timings,
   spring constants, sizes, budgets), what it replaces, what it must never do, how it will be judged.
2. **Build it working.** Tuning numbers live in one config per domain; feel targets are tests that fail on a bad retune.
3. **Feel pass on the running build**, on the target device and camera, never in the head.
4. **Measure.** Film it, step the frames, plot curves, profile, run the checkers.
5. **Blind critic** (section 5). Fix what is confirmed, refilm, review again.
6. **Codify** every new complaint as a checked rule.
7. **Hand over** a fresh playable build under a new name, how to test it, what only a real phone can judge, and for
   taste calls 2-3 variants side by side with your pick. The owner's play time is the queue: batch the calls.
8. **Real players**: funnel events from day one, a 48-hour rollback bar per publish (e.g. first-cohort D1 < 8% or
   60-second bounce > 12%), judged on organic days only.

## 5. Instruments that give an agent eyes and ears

- **Headless lab** running the game's own code at a fixed rate (120 Hz): frames from input to effect, kick peaks,
  settle times, and a **pop check** that fails any frame whose motion fits neither neighbour. Randomness seeded by
  event index so every take is identical and comparable.
- **Film rig inside the game**: stage levels, waves, boss HP, god mode; log telemetry per second (hits/s, the body's
  on-screen box vs every HUD element, frame fill) and **accept or reject takes by script**. Warm the stage first (first
  load 20 fps, then 60); close other heavy apps (they cost 30-60% of the frame rate); new folder per take.
- **Blind reviewer**: a fresh agent or video model that never saw your intent. It gets what is on screen, the 6A
  definition and the axes; "yes" only if every axis ≥ 9; gaps listed worst first with a time and a fix. Phrase it
  harshly ("the owner thinks parts reek of AI slop"). When 4 of 5 independent reviewers name the same flaw unprompted,
  it is real: fix the class. Shuffled before/after pairs without saying which is new catch regressions metrics miss.
  Drop reviews from a weaker fallback model (1-3 points lower, unsupported claims).
- **Know the ceiling**: if the same axes stall for rounds (tactile, art and polish stuck at 6-7 on primitive parts), the
  limit is the asset pipeline, not polish. Propose modelled and rigged art.
- **Look sheet**: fixed eye-height cameras inside and outside, before / after / reference. Never judge by part count.
- **Listening room**: a page that plays the game's actual sound files with Keep/Redo, because the agent cannot hear.
  Fit synthesized sounds to the owner's reference recording by spectrum and envelope (achieved within 1.4 dB and 2.7 dB).
- **Newcomer bot**: a fresh account in a real play session on phone emulation, reading at 3 words/s, tapping the real
  UI, filmed and timed. Real kids take about **1.7x** the bot's times [M]. Filmed runs found ~25 bugs in one tutorial.
  Also test what a **do-nothing player** gets: an AFK player rode helper bots to wave 12.
- **Map simulator**: maps as data, played headless by 12 bot bodies (~1.4 s a round), reporting pace, first contact,
  spawn deaths, dead floor. Calibrated on the owner's recorded play; a change is kept only if it wins on two seed sets.
- **Perf A/B**: measure the old build first (or keep it running beside), freeze the world, fixed cameras, at least 5
  interleaved rounds. Machine drift is ±0.5 ms idle and ±2 ms busy [M]: single numbers are noise.

## 6. Feel numbers that worked

**Fundamentals.** No constant-speed motion; no per-frame `lerp(x, t, 0.1)` (it changes with frame rate). Use
`1 - exp(-k*dt)` or critically damped springs. Step springs at a fixed small step: one 1/30 s step sent a camera
spring to its limits [M]. Input: act on the press frame; buffer a tap 100-130 ms early; coyote time 100 ms; keep a
reload press 350 ms. **Never silently drop an input**: a slam that only accepted presses after 0.28 s of a jump threw
natural taps away, and bots holding the button never noticed. UI: small responses 90-160 ms ease-out, reveals
280-450 ms with 25-40 ms stagger, a badge springs to 1.19x and settles in 0.25 s.

**Gunplay** (battle rifle with real reference stats locked by tests: 625 RPM = 96 ms, 3-shot kill 0.192 s).
- Aim-down-sights 0.25 s on a spring 95% there at 0.167 s, so the last 80 ms are stillness.
- Camera recoil: each shot **sets** the view's velocity (up 15-60°/s, side 25 left to 57.5 right), braked by a
  1,500°/s² centre pull, home counts only 6% [M: 30 rounds climb 6.4°, home in 0.32 s]. A guessed spring climbed
  0.61° and felt "like a laser": copy the real system's rule, not just its numbers.
- Second approach (co-op shooter): aim climbs by exactly the kick in a straight line, delivered over two stages so no
  frame carries more than ~1/5; fall back on a critically damped spring after a hold (pistol 0.08 s / rate 12;
  automatics hold one shot interval + 0.06 s so a burst never sags). Identical at 30/60/144 fps [M]. Organic noise goes
  to camera roll (0.006-0.034 rad/shot), hand kick and reticle bloom, never into the aim.
- Viewmodel: pull back 21 mm (10.5 aimed), muzzle rise 2.3° (0.75°), peak one frame after the shot, home in 125 ms;
  a held burst braces (each kick 86% of the last, floor 62%).
- Muzzle flash 2 frames, its light dead in 50 ms; FOV punch 0.55°/shot; hit-stop on kills only, 45 ms at 12% speed,
  hands and camera only.
- Hit markers: body 1,680 Hz 0.12 s, head 2,480 Hz and gold, kill two rising notes held 0.22 s, multi-kills +2
  semitones a step; damage numbers accumulate and hold 0.85 s. The hit sound is a slap (crack, body, low), not a tick.
- Shotgun spread: a 3D cone (centre, ring of 5, ring of 10), not a flat fan.

**Melee and impact.** Strike: lunge 0.1 s, contact 0.05 s, recoil 0.16 s. Hit-stop as a multiple of contact (x1.3
jab, x1.75 slam, x2.1 double) taken out of the recoil so the attack rate holds. Arm shudder 0.18 studs at 34 Hz;
camera kick 0.55 studs and FOV -5° for 0.14 s. A slam holds ground contact 0.8 s (at 0.55 s the eye missed it),
0.5 s shockwave, 9° FOV kick. Knockback must move the target: a punch that moved 0.9 studs net felt like nothing;
46 studs/s eased over 0.28 s gives ~4 studs a jab, ~18 a three-hit string [M]. A hammer hits everything in its arc
(15 rays over 72° a side, 4 heights); the reticle lights when a swing would land.

**Movement.** Jump 6.2 studs, 0.61 s airtime with three gravities (150 rising, 92 at the apex, 188 falling); the camera
lags 80% of the push on a 3.4 Hz spring. Powerslide 31 → 15.6 studs/s over 0.78 s (sprint 22.8), steers 55°/s, never
blocked by the weapon. FOV widens up to 6° with speed. Head bob by distance (a stride every 9.2 studs), not time.

**Speed sense** (racer). The bike's true speed was right; it did not *read* fast. What reads as speed [M from the
reference]: the camera riding the terrain (frame bob 2.4-2.9% of height, horizon swing ~21% over 2 s from a rolling
road), big objects passing close to the lens ("that parallax is the speed"), smear. Lean from physics
(`tan lean = v² · curvature / g`) on a roll spring of 8.2 rad/s, damping 0.82. Arcade skill: steer into a bend from
0.2 s early to cancel its push; the PERFECT window stays ~0.24 s at every speed, and speed makes bends come faster
rather than the window narrower.

**Gameplay camera.** One boom ceiling everywhere (64 studs; 80 made the worst frames); ease halving the gap every
0.15 s. Turn off raycast hits on every invisible collider (one made the camera wobble). Fade trees, ghost boss
bodies, keep the lens out of hulls, never under the ground (≥ 3.8 studs up).

**Bosses and enemies.** Bosses fail by being quiet, not weak [M]: 13-21 s lulls and single attacks a moving kid walks
out of read as "not boss like". Chain attacks that land under each other's warnings, arena-wide patterns, a third
phase. A boss slower than the player (4 vs 8.1 u/s) reads "like slowmotion"; stalk at 9 (11.7 enraged), lunge after a
0.6 s rear over a red lane at 44 u/s, always escapable by a sidestep during the tell. Hazards at one spot ≥ 0.85 s
apart (a jump is 0.7 s in the air). Later waves add a trick per enemy, not just HP ("they just get more hp").
Ceremonies keep control with the player: boss intro 2.6 s letterbox + name slam; kill slows the world to 0.22x for
0.45 s. Helper bots deal 75-85% damage and can't carry a do-nothing player.

**Rewards.** Every reward travels to where it is counted (coins curve into the counter, a tick per landing, the
counter bumps). Order follows the eye: action, pay lands, JOB DONE, bonus, next goal, upgrade card; never a card
mid-action, never two banners at once. Show each payout once (three "+$" for one payment read as double money).
Reveals 2.3-2.75 s and tappable ("fast + satisfying"); longer is "long and tedious". Effects never wider than 1/5 of
their distance and never over a living body. Every effect group has an off switch.

## 7. Audio

- The agent cannot hear: measure loudness (LUFS), onsets, spectrum; build a listening page; give the owner A/B
  switches (he kept the old synthesized gun sounds over "awful" AI-generated ones).
- Measure the second after a single core sound: reverb, tails and debris ring for ~1 s and ruin it.
- Phones: mix for tiny speakers (52% of one shot's energy sat under 120 Hz, where phones give nothing).
- Music can be keyed to the core action: 96 ms fire interval = a sixteenth at 156.25 BPM; the shot's ring set the key.
- Calm, slower voices beat hype reads. No classical music for an action game.
- Roblox: sound files over ~10 s stream and lose clip onsets (13 of 14 plays) [M]; frame-started sounds jitter 54-139 ms;
  scheduling on the mixer clock is sample-exact.

## 8. Arena level design (12-player shooter, measured on 11 fan-favourite maps at true scale)

- 130-200 m² per player; 70-120 m end to end; a spot sees 8-20% of the map; mid sightline 9-14 m.
- First enemy in sight ≥ 1 s after spawn; < 5% of deaths within 5 s of a spawn; spawns walled from high places.
- ≤ 45% of floor within 1.1 m of a wall or cover; 2-5 passages at every cross-section; 15-25% roofed; walking route
  ≤ 1.2x the straight line.
- From the owner's complaints: 0 fake openings; never two staircases from one room into the same upstairs room; two
  doors into a room ≥ 24 studs apart on one wall; ≤ 3 places to watch in the busiest 90° view; no cover in a lane
  narrower than 28 studs and no gap under 6 beside cover; windows are waist-high parapets you can see, shoot and jump
  through; at most two upstairs rooms that look out.
- Proportions: rooms 12-13 studs clear, doors 8-9, ≥ 8 studs over every walked place. Rooms calm and light, outside vivid.
- Borrow a famous map's 2-3 functions, none of its expression.

## 9. Onboarding and retention (live data)

- **First-minute bounce** [M]: 17% gone by 60 s, 43% by 3 min. Causes found: a 12.4 s loading cover, ~20 buttons and 3
  red badges on the first screen, 6 coach lines in 25 s, a 17.6 s machine "show", first payoff at 2:12. Fix: payoff
  at ~0:45, cover 4-8 s, coaching ≤ 3 words. Removing a mode-picker did not change bounce: the leak moved.
- **The first upgrade leak**: every step held 93%+ until "first payoff → first upgrade", which lost 21-25%. It cost
  $60 at 2:40-4:06; now $15, right after the first sale (priced at what one sale pays).
- **Beginner's skill window**: the perfect zone starts 4x wider and tightens to the real one by attempt 12: first
  PERFECT 4:01 → 2:05, share 40% → 52%, same-age return 10.1% vs 5.4% (p ≈ 0.03) [M].
- **A visible next goal doubled return** (30% vs 15%); daily rewards and a second mode did not [M].
- **Start the run only when the world is whole**: wait for every player to load (caps 15 s / 60 s) behind one loading
  screen ("monsters were already attacking me", "i dont wanna see chopped up stuff").
- **HUD starts with the essentials** and adds each block the first time it matters; one money word; plain words.
- **A tutorial may tease the top (a giant for 25 s) but never its power: speed is earned.**
- **Second live game** [M]: onboarding iteration raised stage-2 entry ~4x (10-16% → 40%), yet D1 stayed ~3% and D7 0%:
  good first minutes do not create a reason to return.
- **Benchmarks** (500+ Roblox games): D1 median 10.3%, top 1% 22.2%; D7 1.6%; sessions 9.8 min; 3.8% of players pay.

**Traffic distorts everything.** Ad cohorts had D1 3-7%; once ads stopped, organic cohorts hit 10-12.6% with no build
change [M]. Keep ads off while measuring. Check denominators: a "25% bounce" was computed on 4 players.

**Roblox discovery** [M]: new games start rated 16+ until 250 highly engaged plays in 60 days (or a refundable
deposit). Home ranks only on players Home itself sent. Home impressions grew 874 → 45k in 12 days, click-through 4-6%
→ 12.8%, play rate 2.6% → 7.9%; Home's own D1 slides as it widens the test pool (normal). Hold big changes 3-5 days
while Home climbs. Read likes per 1,000 visits over days: with one vote per 600-800 visits, a few dislikes swing the
ratio. Roblox has no in-game like prompt; favourites can be prompted (14-20% say yes), so ask at a proud peak.

## 10. Monetization (kids, fair)

- Reality check [M]: one first purchase per ~2,000 new players; shop 962 opens → 146 price taps → 1 buy; a second game
  1,115 opens → 2 buys; a R$5 first offer did nothing (most young kids hold zero Robux). 70% of age-checked players
  were under 13. Next lever: a rewarded ad for a free boost.
- Kid-priced row, cheapest first (R$5/9/19/25/29). Open the store on ~6 featured items (store size does not predict
  rank; day-one breadth confuses), more as weekly drops.
- Robux buys time, capacity and certainty, never luck. Never sell anything that leads to a random roll. One earned
  currency (a 2026 FTC complaint targets layered currencies and false scarcity). No countdowns, no daily purchase
  streaks, always a NO THANKS. The loss screen pays out first, then PLAY AGAIN, then optional spending.
- A free path to every paid convenience. Every claim true. A paid item must be visible and used constantly: a
  R$1,000 golden cleaver was "bought twice, never seen" (a half-stud sliver).
- Test as a stranger, not the owner account with everything unlocked.

## 11. Phone performance (Roblox)

- The 10th-percentile phone fps is the number [M]: it fell 49 → 19 as cheaper phones arrived with a wider audience,
  with no build change. A desktop is no phone proxy: trust script time, object counts and live p10 a day after release.
- **Distance budgets**: particle emitters off past 150 studs, lights past 4x their range, signs past 170, 12%
  hysteresis; lights lit nearest-first by tier (64/36/20, lowest 6). Emitters on 457 → 127, shadow casters 5.4k → 2.6k.
- **Automatic step-down**: one 3 s window under 22 fps drops a tier, under 14 fps drops two. Biggest GPU knobs [M]:
  render quality level 1 −32%, shadows off −20%, hiding near parts under 4 studs −18%; culling far parts gave nothing.
- **Font atlas flicker**: every letter at every size and stroke width shares one atlas, three times over on a
  high-DPI phone. Cap world text boards at 42% of viewport height, whole-pixel strokes (≤ 8), cull far signs.
- **Mesh swap stall**: a body mesh not in memory blocks ~0.19 s, on the server and then on each client; ~700 later
  meshes evict it. Announce, preload on both sides, swap after a short lead.
- **Join size**: 77 MB of art replicated to every client; moving it off clients took the join 39.6 → 2.7 MB; a loading
  cover held only for the first view took the live cover 12.6 → 6.8 s. Count distinct asset ids before promising a
  saving (two areas sharing meshes made a "half the files" promise wrong).
- **Phone crash classes**: highlight effects over bodies (lost renderer), per-frame rescaling of clothed characters.
- **A texture loaded is not a texture drawn**: draw an object invisibly at its real on-screen size before it appears.

**Other engine gotchas** [M]: server NPCs get no default animation script (play animation packs server-side);
scripted cameras bind after the default camera; judge CPU by Heartbeat plus the profiler, never by render time in a
background Studio window (it runs at 15 fps); edit-mode previews lie (no cast shadows, untextured particles), so judge
uploaded assets in a real run; 20k triangles a mesh; freshly uploaded images take minutes to serve.

## 12. Store media: page video, trailers, verticals

- **Framing** [M against a hit game's page video: subject 40-100% of frame height in 20 of 21 shots, median ~65%]: the
  subject (hero plus what it acts on) fills 45-70% of frame height, the hero alone ≥ 25%; centre on the middle third;
  vertical FOV 46-50°; at most two wide shots; slow push-ins, never pull-outs; widen the lens rather than back up.
  Too close ("too zoomed in constantly") and too far (17 of 32 shots with the hero under 15%) both failed.
- **The HUD never covers the body** ("you block the char with the bottom ui"): body at ~47% height, clear of bottom
  bars; log the body box vs HUD per frame and reject takes that overlap.
- **Show the journey, not only peaks**: hook slam, hub, skins, growing waves, upgrades, bosses, co-op, logo.
- **Grammar**: one claim of ≤ 7 words inside 10 s; first 3 s carry the hook; frame 0 is the thumbnail; claim then
  proof, repeated; 40-60% holds, something new every 2-4 s; shake ≤ 3 px and only on impacts; cut on the beat grid
  (120 BPM at 30 fps = 15 frames a beat). "If a viewer can name the effect, it is too strong."
- **Acted staged shots beat filmed live fights** (bodies and trees block close cameras); keyed spline cameras, real
  slow motion from high-fps re-takes (120 fps, never AI-interpolated).
- **Flicker in an edit is usually the edit** [M, four rounds]: overlays timed 1/60 + 0.002 s drew twice; fractional
  speeds (1.5x, 1.9x) judder, use integers; punch-zooms; a game light pulsing per event; a gameplay camera with its own
  sway. Keep zoom ≤ 0.8% a frame; beat-synced smooth swells are welcome, random punches are not.
- **Verticals**: a centre crop keeps ~45% of the frame ("you don't see anything"); film with a rolled camera and wider
  lens, check a side-by-side sheet against the 16:9 at the same timestamps, hide name labels that lie sideways.
- **Audio for kids** ("its not made for human children"): one narrator, one piece of music that grows (not 7 in 38 s),
  words ≥ 10 dB over music in the 1-4 kHz band, speaking ~1/3 of the time and never over a big hit, ≤ 1 cue a second,
  lean low end; master −14 LUFS, true peak −1.2 dBTP.
- **Roblox page video**: real gameplay only, ≤ 60 s, no narration or call to action; it autoplays on Home (+41%
  recommended plays in Roblox's test). OCR the cut for real account names.
- **Thumbnails**: in-game crops top out around 6/10; paint key art from verified references, then crop and letter.
- **TikTok**: posts 3-5 of a session got no reach [M]; the call to action names the game and platform, never "search".

## 13. Art pipeline

- Write models as code (Blender Python) and bake; one skeleton with code-driven motion under every look.
- Whole AI-generated models work only when already hard pieces (machines); soft bodies needed repair rounds, and
  generators bake light and stains into textures. Automated likeness scores are advisory: the owner chose the
  hand-built remodel over a higher-scoring AI model on looks.
- Match measurably: silhouette overlap 29% → 88.5% in six rounds beat eyeballing.
- Restyle freely, keep the stats. Generate on paid plans (free tiers can carry attribution licences).

## 14. Working with an owner who has taste

- Read a burst of messages whole before building; when words name a stat or control, confirm which one ("ADS speed"
  meant strafe speed while aiming, not aim time).
- "1:1" means shape, proportion and feel in the game's own clean style; show versions side by side.
- Compare material the owner hands over against what is built before swapping it in (a "fix" he sent was identical
  to the build; the real fix was a strength slider).
- Keep old versions playable beside reworks, with a pick in the UI; make big changes live tuning knobs.
- Merging, publishing and spending are the owner's call. Rapid progress over rigorous testing: test hard only where a
  mistake is expensive or invisible, and bring batched judgement calls, not reports.

## 15. About prompting for quality

"6A" in a prompt works only after its meaning is written down, as above; piling on adjectives stops helping fast.
What moves results: a concrete reference, explicit numbers, and instruments that show the running build. A separate
polish pass works because it acts on something filmed and measured. Asking a model to "imagine a draft and discard
it" does not replace a reviewer who never saw its reasoning. Maximalist instructions ("layer every effect",
"implement every implied detail", "never ship good enough") produce exactly the slop in section 3: restraint, small
scope and the player's first ten minutes are the bar.
