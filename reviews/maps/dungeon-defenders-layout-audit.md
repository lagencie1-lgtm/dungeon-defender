# Dungeon Defenders map references and asset audit

24 November 2026 · Heroes Tower Defense

**Start with Cursed Forest.** Connect its existing keep, chapel, and portal shrine so players can spot a good build position and cut across to intercept a wave. A stronger keep silhouette and a continuous bank will change how the map reads on a phone. The painted map kit already covers most of the construction. Magma Fortress is the next candidate for a second enemy approach because it already has several river crossings.

## What players returned to

DunDefPlanner’s five most submitted maps in the 24 November snapshot were **Infested Ruins (336 builds), The Striking Tree (209), Wintermire (182), Omenak (173), and The Tinkerer's Lab (161)**. Build submissions measure how often players publish strategies. Progression and loot farms contribute to those totals. Infested Ruins is a good example because players publish dedicated armor-farming setups for it. [DunDefPlanner map index](https://dundefplanner.com/maps.php) · [Infested Ruins player farming guide](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=lhayVy1nG28)

Chromatic Games described **Foundries and Forges** as a fan favorite when announcing its Awakened remake. A longtime **Glitterhelm Caverns** player recalled spending most of their playtime there, and a group returning to the game on PC named **The Ramparts** as their favorite. These give us examples of affection for maps with relatively few submitted builds. ## Existing map kit and assets to improve

The repo has **24 painted map meshes**, eight pieces across each of the forest, magma, and crystal themes. The kit includes ruined walls, low battlements, arches, broken pillars, bridges, retaining walls, and stairs. The eighth piece is specific to each theme: standing stones, a brazier, or a crystal cart. Painted ground materials, collision boxes for the architecture, and fading for tall pieces that hide the hero are already in place. The retaining wall kit includes a flagstone rim that covers the edge of raised terrain, and its stairs run alongside the wall. ## First pass on Cursed Forest

1. **Keep lookout.** Frame the first view with the broken keep and make the bridge visible from the hero’s starting position. Use the taller tower for orientation and keep the approach clear around the stairs.
2. **Chapel yard.** Keep a wide, flat combat area and a recognizable build position. Connect the keep and chapel with a short hero footpath so a player can see a wave advance and get ahead of it. Keep small props grouped at the well or along the wall.
3. **Shrine bridge.** Make the bridge the last clear interception point. Put buildable ground beside its landing and leave a broad space on the far side for players to move and fight near the portal.

Use the current enemy path for this first pass. In a fresh phone-size run, check that a new player can see the portal objective, recognize one useful build spot, and use the shortcut during a wave. Reuse the map tour checks for flat buildable ground, blocked water, stairs, lane clearance, and camera visibility. Confirm the shortcut still makes sense with enemies and defenders in view. ## Second enemy approach in Magma Fortress

Each map currently declares one enemy path. Spawning, movement, and placement clearance use that path. A second real route needs a way to assign enemies to an entrance, move each enemy on its assigned route, reserve both lanes from building, and show players which entrance is active. Existing walls and river crossings can make the routes readable. The map review records that defenders currently shoot through walls, so check how much ground one tower position covers before treating a wall as a tactical divider. Test an early wave from one entrance and a later wave from both before expanding the design.
